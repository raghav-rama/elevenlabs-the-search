## Visual rules

- Anchor architecture to the approved station and actual ending frame of the selected transformation: layered glass towers, dark brushed metal, elevated transit, skybridges and bevelled structural details.
- Keep bright hazy daylight throughout. Cyan marks transit/technology, magenta provides restrained environmental accents and warm gold links practical lights to Riley's dress. Avoid switching between a blue night city and an ordinary sunny suburb.
- Preserve realistic skin, the selected asymmetric facial cyberware and thin body inlays, gold dress and tall boots. Exact character references take priority over generic cyberpunk adjectives.
- Retain lightly damp reflective paving and real materials. Civilians, shopfronts and vehicles belong to the same near-future world. A neon sign alone will not make a conventional street match the station.
- Put exact brand/product labels into the edit. For intelligence, reserve image space for a stable editorial HUD; do not depend on a generator preserving fine typography.

## Overhead staging

Use the interactive layout in [the reference board](index.html). Both subjects move toward image-top. The camera follows their shared travel, while Riley gains on the nose in a separate lane to image-left. Road/rail structure moving down-frame supplies forward travel cues.

Begin about one body length behind the nose. Pass it near 40.5 seconds, during “trying to catch you now.” By the ending, the trailing boot must clear the nose by at least one body length; hold the readable lead through the spiral cut. No last-frame-only victory, sideways lane drift or head-on geometry.

Frame the train nose and only part of its lead car. Riley should occupy roughly 12–16 percent of image height while retaining believable relative size. The target is clearer framing, not enlarging her into a giant. Rails remain continuous beneath the train, both headings align and the running lane has a visible divider and lateral clearance. The diagram is explanatory and not a scale drawing or generated production frame.
